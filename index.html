What we are tracking

You are recommended for the one question your website answers in plain words, and absent from every question it does not.

There are two different doors into your shop, and they no longer open together.

For years being findable meant one thing: showing up high in Google’s list of blue links. That still matters, and plenty of your customers shop exactly that way.

But a growing number never see that list. They ask an assistant a question in plain English and get back one answer, with a few makers named in it. Being named in that answer is a different thing from showing up high in Google’s list, and the two have come apart. Being strong in one does not carry you in the other.

So this section has two parts. The first asks: do assistants name you? The second asks: where do you sit in Google’s list? You are a striking example of the two disagreeing, and that disagreement is exactly where the opportunity is.

Four words used below, in plain terms. Being named is when an assistant mentions you anywhere in its answer. Being picked is stronger: the assistant puts you forward as the answer, not as one of a list. A listing is one time your page appeared in somebody’s Google results. Your spot is how far down that Google list you were, where 1st to 10th is the first page and almost nobody scrolls past it. A click from Google is somebody seeing your listing and choosing to open it.

One honest limit on that clicks number. This table can only see Google. It does not mean nobody else reached you: when an assistant names you and somebody comes straight to your shop, Google never sees that visit at all. Some people click straight through and your own site analytics catch them, but plenty read the answer and then simply type your name into their browser, which arrives looking identical to somebody who already knew you. This is genuinely hard to count, and anybody who says they can measure it precisely is selling something. In practice it means the effect of being named is almost certainly larger than any number here can show, not smaller.

The finding that explains both tables. Your home page says Sunshine Coast, in plain words, on the page. That is the one question the assistants get right about you. Fifteen of your seventeen gemstone collection pages have no words on them at all, so when somebody asks about moonstone or turquoise or opal, there is nothing on your site for a machine to read and repeat. The pattern is that exact, and it is the whole case for the work ahead.

You are not short of writing, and this is the encouraging part. All 304 of your products carry a description, and eight of your story collections carry your own writing, some of it 800 characters and genuinely lovely. The gap is not ability. It is that the writing never reached the gemstone pages, which are exactly the ones buyers search for.

Turquoise is the clearest opening you have. Google already puts your turquoise guide in the top ten, and around forty different turquoise questions bring up your pages between the 5th and 12th spot. Yet not one AI tool names you when somebody asks for a turquoise specialist. The reputation is already built. It simply is not reaching the assistants, and that is a much easier problem than starting from nothing.

Your biggest near-miss on Google. Your guide on the difference between solid gold, gold filled, gold vermeil and gold plated appeared 1,644 times in six days, sitting around the 7th spot, and was opened once. People are being shown it and choosing not to click, which almost always means the title and description in the results are not pulling their weight. That is a small fix with a large number behind it.

Two honest limits. Six days is a short window, so treat the direction as real and the exact figures as provisional until next month. And these AI answers are one measurement on one day; assistants vary their wording every time they are asked, so what we watch over time is whether your name keeps coming back, not whether any single answer repeats.
